OpenStand

Privacy policy

Effective 25 October 2026

  • No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking, no cookies.
  • Your library is stored in your browser, on your device.
  • If you turn on sync, your library is encrypted on your device and stored where you choose. We can't read it, and neither can your storage provider.

This policy covers OpenStand at openstand.rodlabs.dev. “We” means RodLabs, which runs that site. OpenStand is open source, so anyone can run their own copy; those copies are run by others, and this policy doesn't cover them.

Your library stays on your device

Your songs, files, annotations, setlists, play history and settings are stored in your browser's storage on your device (IndexedDB and local storage). The app itself is cached on your device so it works offline. Nothing in your library is uploaded unless you turn on library sync. Backups, song packages and exports are files that you save or share yourself.

No tracking

OpenStand contains no analytics, advertising, telemetry or error-reporting code, and it sets no cookies. We don't collect, sell or share information about you or your music.

Hosting

The site runs on Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare handles your IP address and basic request details (such as the page requested, your browser and the time) to deliver the site and protect it from abuse, under Cloudflare's privacy policy. Cloudflare shows us aggregate traffic statistics, such as request counts by country.

We keep no per-request logs of the relay or the sign-in helper described below. Cloudflare still handles those requests as our host, as above, and shows us aggregate statistics such as request counts and error rates. If our code fails, or writes a short diagnostic note (for the sign-in helper, at most the provider, the kind of request and the status of the answer), Cloudflare keeps that message for up to 7 days so we can fix the problem. These messages never contain sign-in codes, tokens, account details or the messages passed through the relay.

Library sync (optional)

Library sync keeps your library the same on all your devices. Your devices exchange it through storage that you choose: your own S3-compatible bucket (such as Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, Amazon S3 or MinIO), or a folder in your Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive. Your browser talks to that storage directly; your library never passes through our servers.

  • End-to-end encrypted. Your first device creates a random library key. Songs, files, file names, annotations and settings are encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before they're uploaded. We don't have the key.
  • What your storage provider can see: encrypted files, how many there are, their sizes (close to the size of each original file) and when your devices read and write them, plus your IP address. From sizes alone, a provider could guess that you have a particular file whose exact size it already knows.
  • Keys stay on your devices. The library key, S3 access keys and sign-in tokens are stored only in the browser on each of your devices.

Dropbox, Google Drive and OneDrive

When you connect one of these, you sign in on the provider's own page and give OpenStand limited access. Each device signs in separately, with the same account.

  • Dropbox: its own app folder only (Apps/OpenStand), to list, read and write files in it (files.metadata.read, files.content.read, files.content.write), plus your basic account information (account_info.read).
  • Google Drive: only files that OpenStand creates (drive.file), kept in a folder named “OpenStand” in your My Drive. OpenStand can't see your other Drive files. With this access, Google Drive also tells OpenStand your account's name and email address.
  • OneDrive: its own app folder only (Apps/OpenStand, Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder), plus offline_access so it stays connected without asking you to sign in again, and openid, profile and email to see which account you signed in with. Microsoft may also list its basic “Sign you in and read your profile” permission (User.Read).

OpenStand reads your account's name and email address, and how much storage space you have, to show you which account is connected and to make sure all your devices use the same one. These details stay in the browser on your devices; the email address and an account id from the provider are also part of pairing codes (see below). Otherwise OpenStand uses this access only to store and fetch your encrypted library. It doesn't read your other files, contacts or messages.

Dropbox sign-in happens entirely between your browser and Dropbox. Google and Microsoft require the app's private client secret to issue and renew sign-in tokens, and that secret can't be put in a web page. So for Google Drive and OneDrive, your browser sends the one-time sign-in code, and later the renewal token, through our sign-in helper at openstand.rodlabs.dev/api/oauth. The helper adds the secret, passes the request to Google or Microsoft and returns the answer to your browser; for Microsoft, that answer includes your account's name and email address. It doesn't store or log codes, tokens or account details.

Google user data

OpenStand's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Access: only the files OpenStand creates in your Google Drive, plus your account's name, email address and storage usage, which Google Drive provides with that access.
  • Use: only to sync your encrypted library between your devices, a feature you turn on yourself, and to show which Google account is connected and check that all your devices use the same one.
  • Storage: the encrypted library is stored in your Google Drive. The sign-in tokens and your account's name, email address and id are stored in the browser on your devices, and the email address and id are also part of pairing codes. Sign-in codes and tokens pass through our sign-in helper without being stored. We keep no Google user data on our servers.
  • Sharing: we don't transfer Google user data to anyone. We don't sell it, use it for advertising or use it to train AI models. No person at OpenStand reads it, and we couldn't: it's encrypted with a key we don't have.

The relay

The relay at openstand.rodlabs.dev/relay passes short messages between devices that are online at the same time. It keeps them in memory only long enough to forward them and never stores them. It sees IP addresses, room names and when messages are sent. It's used in two ways:

  • Library sync sends a small encrypted notice after each change, so your other devices fetch it within seconds. The room is a random id derived from your library key. Without the relay, sync still works by checking your storage every 30 seconds.
  • Page sync (if you choose “Use this site” under Settings → Devices & sync) mirrors page turns between devices in a room you name. These messages are not encrypted: they contain the device name you set, the titles and artists of the songs you open, setlist names and page positions. Anyone who joins the same room name sees them, so pick a name that's hard to guess, or run your own relay.

Pairing codes

To add a device to sync, OpenStand shows a QR code or a text code. It contains your library key and where the library is stored: for S3 storage, your storage access keys; for Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive, the provider, your account's email address and account id (so the new device can ask you to sign in with the same account) and, for Google Drive, the id of the OpenStand folder. It never contains Dropbox, Google or Microsoft sign-in tokens. It only goes from your screen to your other device's camera, or through your clipboard; OpenStand never sends it anywhere. Treat it like a password.

Camera, MIDI and pedals

  • Camera: used only when you scan a page, scan a pairing code or turn on face gestures, after your browser asks for permission. Video is processed on your device and is never recorded or uploaded.
  • MIDI devices and Bluetooth pedals are read on your device. Pedals work like a keyboard. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Downloads from other sites

A few optional features download files from other services the first time you use them. These are plain downloads: none of your data is sent, but like any website, the service sees your IP address and browser details.

  • Face gestures: the MediaPipe runtime from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) and the face model from Google (storage.googleapis.com).
  • ABC notation playback: instrument sounds from paulrosen.github.io (GitHub Pages).
  • Your own links: images in ChordPro files and audio tracks added by URL are loaded from the addresses you enter.

Deleting your data

  • On a device: delete songs or libraries in the app, or clear this site's data in your browser settings.
  • Synced data: Settings → Library sync → Delete synced data from storage, or delete the folder or bucket yourself.
  • Signing a device out: Settings → Library sync → Stop syncing deletes that device's sign-in tokens (for Dropbox, it also cancels them). It doesn't remove OpenStand's access to your Google or Microsoft account, because that would sign out your other devices too.
  • To disconnect OpenStand completely, remove its access in your account settings: Google, Microsoft (work or school accounts: My Apps) or Dropbox.

We don't hold your library or any account for you, so there is nothing for us to export or delete on our side.

Children

OpenStand has no accounts and doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.
